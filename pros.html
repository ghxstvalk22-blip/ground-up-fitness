Train like a pro

The habits Olympia champions and record holders share, scaled down for your first year in the gym.

  1. 01

    Progressive overload

    Every champion physique was built one small jump at a time: a little more weight, or one more rep, than last time.

    How to start: progressive overload
    1. Write down every working set: the exercise, the weight, and the reps. A notes app or the log on the Programs page both work.
    2. Pick a rep range, like 8 to 12. When you hit the top of the range on every set, add the smallest jump the gym has next time (usually 5 lb, or 2.5 lb per side on light lifts).
    3. If you can't add weight, add a rep. One more rep than last time still counts as progress.
    4. Expect slower jumps after a few months. Small steady gains beat big jumps you can't hold with good form.

    Common mistake: adding weight every session no matter what, until form breaks down.

    Start with: write down every set
  2. 02

    Consistency for years

    Pro physiques take many years of training. The plan you stick with beats the perfect plan you quit in March.

    How to start: consistency
    1. Pick three days you can actually keep, like Monday, Wednesday, and Friday, and put them in your calendar like appointments.
    2. Have a short version ready for busy days: just the first two exercises. A 20-minute workout keeps the habit alive.
    3. Missed a day? Do the next workout on the next day you can. Don't try to cram two in one.
    4. Look back every month, not every day. Progress is easier to see in your log than in the mirror.

    Common mistake: starting with six days a week and burning out by week three.

    Start with: three fixed days a week
  3. 03

    Hard sets, clean reps

    The pros push their working sets close to failure, but they control every rep. Sloppy reps build injuries, not muscle.

    How to start: hard sets
    1. Use the “reps left in the tank” test: end most sets when you could still do 1 to 3 more clean reps.
    2. Lower the weight under control (about two seconds) and don't bounce it at the bottom.
    3. If your form changes to finish a rep, the set is over. That's the rep that leads to injuries.
    4. Keep true all-out sets for safer machine and dumbbell exercises, not heavy squats or deadlifts without a spotter.

    Common mistake: stopping every set when it first feels hard, long before the muscles are really working.

    Start with: how heavy to go
  4. 04

    Eat for the goal

    Protein every day, and calories matched to what you're after: a little less to lose fat, a little more to build muscle.

    How to start: eating for the goal
    1. Build each meal around a protein food you like: chicken, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt, tofu, beans, or lean meat.
    2. Use the calorie estimator for a starting point, then watch your weekly average weight for two to three weeks and adjust by small amounts.
    3. To lose fat, aim for about 0.5 to 1% of your body weight per week. To build muscle, gain slowly, about 1 to 2 lb a month.
    4. Fill half your plate with vegetables or fruit, and drink water through the day.

    Talk to a doctor or registered dietitian before changing how you eat if you have a health condition, take medication, or are pregnant.

    Start with: your calorie and protein targets
  5. 05

    Recovery is training

    Muscle grows between workouts, not during them. Seven to nine hours of sleep and real rest days are part of the program.

    How to start: recovery
    1. Aim for 7 to 9 hours of sleep. Going to bed and waking up at the same time each day helps the most.
    2. Leave at least one day between hard sessions for the same muscles. A full-body plan three days a week does this for you.
    3. On rest days, walk or stretch lightly. Easy movement helps you feel less stiff.
    4. Some soreness is normal at first. Sharp pain, or pain that gets worse during a set, is a signal to stop and get it checked.

    Every fourth week, a lighter week helps your body catch up. Most plans on this site build this in.

    Start with: training when you're sore
  6. 06

    Master the basics

    Strip away the highlight reels and pro programs still center on squats, presses, rows, and hinges, done well, for years.

    How to start: the basics
    1. Learn one move from each group: a squat, a hinge (like a Romanian deadlift), a push (like a bench or push-up), and a pull (like a row or pulldown).
    2. Start lighter than you think, even with just the bar or a light dumbbell, and film a set from the side to check your form.
    3. Keep the same main lifts for a few months so you can see them improve. Change the smaller exercises if you get bored.
    4. Each exercise in the exercise library has step-by-step how-tos and a video search to compare against.

    Common mistake: jumping between new programs every week and never getting good at any lift.

    Start with: your first two weeks

A reality check: top professional physiques come from many years of work, genetics, and in many cases performance-enhancing drugs. Use them for inspiration and ideas, and compare yourself only to last month's you.
