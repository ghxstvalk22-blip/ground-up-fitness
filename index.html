Pick your starting point

Every journey here starts the same way: a plan you can actually follow, done consistently. Choose the goal that sounds most like you.

Goal 01

Lose weight

Fat loss comes from eating a little less than you burn. Lifting makes sure what you lose is fat, not the muscle that keeps you strong.

  1. Estimate your daily calories and protein.
  2. Lift 3 to 4 days a week.
  3. Walk every day, even on rest days.
  4. Weigh in a few mornings a week and watch the trend, not the single day.
Estimate my calories See the fat loss plan
Goal 02

Get stronger

Strength is a skill you practice. A few big lifts, done often, with a little more weight over time, is how almost everyone gets strong.

  1. Learn the squat, bench, deadlift, and overhead press.
  2. Start lighter than you think you need to.
  3. Add a small amount of weight each week.
  4. Write down every session so you know what to beat.
Estimate my max See the strength plan
Goal 03

I'm brand new

Nobody at the gym is watching you as closely as you think. Your only job for the first month is to show up and learn the movements.

  1. Week 1: go 3 times. Use light weights and learn each exercise.
  2. Week 2: same plan, add a little weight where it felt easy.
  3. Pick the same days and times each week so it becomes routine.
  4. Read the gym basics below before your first session.

Train like a pro

What the pros have in common

Olympia champions and world-record lifters don't share one secret routine. They share habits. Here's what those habits look like, scaled down for your first year in the gym.

  1. 01

    Progressive overload

    Every champion physique was built one small jump at a time: a little more weight, or one more rep, than last time.

    Start with: write down every set →
  2. 02

    Consistency for years

    Pro physiques take many years of training. The plan you stick with beats the perfect plan you quit in March.

    Start with: three fixed days a week →
  3. 03

    Hard sets, clean reps

    The pros push their working sets close to failure, but they control every rep. Sloppy reps build injuries, not muscle.

    Start with: how heavy to go →
  4. 04

    Eat for the goal

    Protein every day, and calories matched to what you're after: a little less to lose fat, a little more to build muscle.

    Start with: your calorie and protein targets →
  5. 05

    Recovery is training

    Muscle grows between workouts, not during them. Seven to nine hours of sleep and real rest days are part of the program.

    Start with: training when you're sore →
  6. 06

    Master the basics

    Strip away the highlight reels and pro programs still center on squats, presses, rows, and hinges, done well, for years.

    Start with: your first two weeks →

A reality check: top professional physiques come from many years of work, genetics, and in many cases performance-enhancing drugs. Use them for inspiration and ideas, and compare yourself only to last month's you.

Gym basics

The questions almost everyone has in their first few months.

How do I know what weight to use?

Pick a weight you can lift for the full number of reps with good form, finishing each set feeling like you could have done two or three more. If you could have done six more, go heavier next set. If your form broke down, go lighter.

How often should I train?

Three days a week is plenty to make great progress, especially at the start. Four is good if you enjoy it and recover well. Consistency beats volume: three sessions every week for a year will do more than six sessions a week for a month.

Will lifting make me bulky?

Not by accident. Building a lot of muscle takes years of focused training and eating more food on purpose. For most people, lifting makes them look leaner and feel stronger.

Cardio or weights for losing fat?

Both help, but neither replaces eating a little less. Weights protect your muscle while you lose. Walking and cardio burn extra energy and are good for your heart. The plans here use both.

I'm sore. Should I skip my workout?

Mild soreness is normal in the first few weeks and usually fades once you warm up. Train anyway, maybe a little lighter. Sharp pain, joint pain, or pain that gets worse as you move is different: stop that exercise and get it checked.

What are the unwritten gym rules?

Put your weights back when you're done. Wipe down benches and machines. Don't stand right in front of someone using a mirror or rack. If someone is between sets on equipment you want, it's fine to ask how many sets they have left or whether you can work in.

How much protein do I need?

A common target for people who lift is about 0.7 to 1 gram per pound of body weight (1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilogram). If you have a lot of weight to lose, base it on your goal weight instead. The calorie estimator above calculates this for you.
