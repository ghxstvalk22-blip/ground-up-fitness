Goal 01

I'm brand new

Nobody at the gym is watching you as closely as you think. Your only job for the first month is to show up and learn the movements.

  1. Week 1: go 3 times. Use light weights and learn each exercise.
  2. Week 2: same plan, add a little weight where it felt easy.
  3. Pick the same days and times each week so it becomes routine.
  4. Read the first-day guide below before you go.

Goal 02

Lose weight

Fat loss comes from eating a little less than you burn. Lifting makes sure what you lose is fat, not the muscle that keeps you strong.

  1. Estimate your daily calories and protein.
  2. Lift 3 to 4 days a week.
  3. Walk every day, even on rest days.
  4. Weigh in a few mornings a week and watch the trend, not the single day.

Goal 03

Get stronger

Strength is a skill you practice. A few big lifts, done often, with a little more weight over time, is how almost everyone gets strong.

  1. Learn the squat, bench, deadlift, and overhead press.
  2. Start lighter than you think you need to.
  3. Add a small amount of weight each week.
  4. Write down every session so you know what to beat.

Your first day

Start to finish, no surprises

Feeling nervous is normal. Most people in the gym felt exactly the same way once. Here's the whole first visit, so nothing catches you off guard.

  1. The night before

    Pack your bag

    Your checkmarks are saved on this device.

  2. 1 to 2 hours before

    Eat something small

    A normal meal or a snack with some carbs and protein, like yogurt and fruit, or a sandwich. Training hungry or right after a huge meal both feel bad.

  3. When you walk in

    Ask for a tour

    Most gyms will show you around for free. Ask where the dumbbells, benches, and cable machines are, and how to adjust the machines on your plan. Staff would much rather show you than watch you guess.

  4. First 5 minutes

    Warm up

    Do the 5-minute warm-up below. It gets your body ready and gives you a minute to settle in.

  5. The next 40 minutes

    Follow Day 1, light

    Open Day 1 of the beginner plan. Use weights that feel easy today; you're learning the movement, not testing yourself. Tap How to on any exercise if you're unsure. If a machine is taken, tap Swap, or ask "how many sets do you have left?" That's a normal gym question.

  6. After

    Write it down, then go home proud

    Log what you lifted so the plan can tell you what to do next time. Drink some water. Feeling a bit sore for a day or two is normal; sharp or joint pain is not, so get that checked.

Before every workout

The 5-minute warm-up

Short, simple, and it makes your first sets feel a lot better.

  1. 3 minutes of easy cardio. Bike, rower, or a brisk walk. You should be able to talk.
  2. Move everything once. 10 bodyweight squats, 10 arm circles each way, 10 , and 10 slow .
  3. Ramp up on your first exercise. Before your working sets, do a light set of 10, then about half your working weight for 5, then about three-quarters for 3. Then start the plan's sets.

If you have an injury or a health condition, check with a doctor or physical therapist before you start a new program.

Gym words, decoded

What everyone's talking about

The plan uses a few words that nobody explains. Here they are.

Rep
One time through a movement. One squat down and up is one rep.
Set
A group of reps done back to back. "3 × 10" means 3 sets of 10 reps.
Rest
The break between sets. Bigger lifts need longer rests (2 to 3 minutes); small ones need less (about 1 minute).
Reps in the tank
How many more reps you could have done. "Stop with 2 in the tank" means end the set when you could still do about 2 more with good form.
Progressive overload
Doing a little more over time: a bit more weight, or one more rep. It's how everyone gets stronger.
Compound
An exercise that uses several joints and big muscles at once, like a squat, press, or row.
Isolation
An exercise that focuses on one muscle, like a curl or a lateral raise.
Failure
The point where you can't do another rep with good form. Beginners don't need to train to failure.
Superset
Two exercises done back to back with no rest in between, then a rest.
PR
Personal record: the most weight or reps you've ever done on a lift.
Deload
A lighter week, about half the usual sets, to let your body recover. Useful every 6 to 10 weeks or when you feel run down.
Working in
Taking turns on equipment with someone between your sets. Ask "mind if I work in?" It's normal.
Spotter
Someone who stands by to help if you get stuck on a lift, usually the bench press. Machines and safety bars do the same job when you train alone.
Form
How you do the movement. Good form first, then add weight.
